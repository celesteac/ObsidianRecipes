The secret to keeping fried chicken crunchy under a wet, sugary glaze lies in **minimizing gluten formation** and **reducing internal moisture in the crust**.

**Ingredient Upgrades**

- **Cornstarch or Potato Starch (50% to 100% of dredge):** All-purpose flour develops gluten when wet, creating a spongy structure that rapidly absorbs sauce. Pure starches contain no gluten and form a glass-like, rigid shell that resists moisture penetration.
- **Rice Flour:** Replacing 20–30% of your flour with white rice flour creates an airy, brittle crunch (standard in Korean fried chicken).
- **Baking Powder:** Adding 1/2 teaspoon per cup of dredge raises the pH and creates microscopic carbon dioxide bubbles during frying. This adds surface area and micro-pockets that stay shatteringly crisp.
- **Vodka or High-Proof Alcohol:** If using a wet batter, substitute part of the water with vodka. Alcohol evaporates much faster than water in hot oil, dehydrating the crust faster and preventing gluten from forming.

**Crucial Techniques**

- **The Double-Fry Method:**
    - **First fry (325°F / 163°C for 3–4 mins):** Cooks the meat through and drives out inner moisture.
    - **Rest (5–10 mins on a wire rack):** Allows remaining steam to migrate to the surface.
    - **Second fry (375°F–390°F / 190°C–200°C for 1–2 mins):** Instantly vaporizes surface moisture, creating a hard, blistered shell that stays crunchy far longer.
- **Drain on a Wire Rack (Never Paper Towels):** Paper towels trap escaping steam underneath the pieces, softening the bottom crust within seconds.
- **Thicken the Sauce Properly:** A watery sauce will immediately ruin a crust. Ensure the orange sauce is cooked down with enough cornstarch slurry so it coats the back of a spoon with a glossy, syrup-like consistency.
- **Flash-Toss Off the Heat:** Never simmer fried chicken in the sauce. Bring the sauce to a bubbling glaze in the pan, turn the heat completely **off**, dump in the hot chicken, and toss for just 15–20 seconds to coat.
